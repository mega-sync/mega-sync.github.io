Mega alternatives: six cloud storage services and how they differ

These Mega alternatives are six cloud storage services with their own desktop sync. For each one, the free storage, the encryption model and the plan sizes come from that vendor's own plans page.

Updated 2026-11-06 · 4 min read · By Cloud Sync Handbook

The closest Mega alternatives are Proton Drive, Sync.com and Internxt, which also describe their storage as end to end or zero knowledge encrypted. pCloud, Dropbox and Google Drive are mainstream options with different encryption models. None of the six lists a free tier larger than Mega's 20 GB.

This is an independent guide, not Mega's website. Buttons lead to the official Mega site and may be affiliate links; if they are, the price you pay does not change. Facts were checked on 6 November 2026 against Mega's public GitHub repositories and its App Store listing.

How this list was built

These Mega alternatives were chosen because each offers cloud storage with a desktop client, which is the job the Mega desktop app does. The facts for each service were read on its own plans page on 6 November 2026. Some pages showed prices in euros and some in dollars; the currency is kept as shown. Nothing here is based on tests.

For Mega's side of the comparison, see the review for features and limits and the pricing page for plans.

Privacy first alternatives

Proton Drive gives 5 GB free and lists Drive Plus with 200 GB at $4.99 a month. Its page describes end to end encryption with a design in which Proton itself cannot view files, and lists apps for Windows and macOS plus a command line client. It suits people who want a small, private drive.

Sync.com describes its platform as end to end encrypted. The page read for this list covers team plans of 1 TB, 2 TB and 10 TB per user with a three user minimum; plans for individuals are on a separate page that was not read.

Internxt gives 1 GB free and sells Essential, Premium and Ultimate plans with 1 TB, 2 TB and 5 TB. It describes zero knowledge AES 256 encryption applied on the device before upload, lists post quantum encryption as a feature, and names Linux, Windows and macOS for its desktop app. It also offers lifetime plans.

Mainstream alternatives

pCloud is known for lifetime plans: 500 GB, 2 TB and 10 TB for a one time payment, shown at 219, 499 and 1499 euros on the day of checking, each marked as reduced from a higher list price. Its plans page describes protection in transit and 256 bit AES encryption.

Dropbox Basic is free with 2 GB. Plus is the paid plan for one person, shown at 9.99 euros a month. The plans page lists end to end encryption only in the Advanced team plan.

Google Drive storage is sold through Google One. Every Google account has 15 GB free, the Basic plan adds 100 GB at 1.99 euros a month, and larger tiers of 2 TB and 5 TB are listed. The plans page does not discuss end to end encryption.

When Mega is still the better fit

Mega keeps three advantages in this group on published facts: the largest free tier at 20 GB, a zero knowledge model on every plan including the free one, and an officially supported Linux desktop client for five distributions. Its desktop app source code is public as well.

If those points matter to you, start with the free plan. The download page has the current version and install steps, and the system requirements list the minimum Windows, macOS and Linux versions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Mega?

For a similar privacy model, Proton Drive, Sync.com and Internxt all describe end to end or zero knowledge encryption. Which one fits depends on how much storage you need and which desktop systems you use.

Which Mega alternative has the most free storage?

Among the six services listed, Google Drive has the largest free tier at 15 GB. Mega's own free plan is larger at 20 GB.

Is there a Mega alternative with a lifetime plan?

Yes. pCloud lists lifetime plans of 500 GB, 2 TB and 10 TB, and Internxt also offers lifetime plans. Mega's listing shows monthly and yearly billing only.

Which alternatives have a Linux desktop app?

Internxt names Linux, Windows and macOS for its desktop app. Proton Drive's page lists Windows, macOS and a command line client.

Is Dropbox end to end encrypted like Mega?

Not on every plan. Dropbox's plans page lists 256 bit AES and SSL/TLS encryption on all plans and end to end encryption only in its Advanced team plan.
